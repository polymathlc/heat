2 What is a source of heat?

☀️ The Sun & Uses of Heat

5 What is the main source of heat on Earth?

We have learnt that the Sun is the main source of light on Earth. The Sun is also the Earth's main source of heat energy.

The sun provides heat for living things to maintain their body temperatures in order to survive. Heat from the sun also allows water to evaporate and condense, providing living things with a constant supply of fresh water.

6 What are some examples of the activities in which humans use heat for?

The following are some activities that humans use heat for:

1. To dry our clothes.
2. To cook food.
3. To kill germs and bacteria.
4. To maintain body temperature in cold countries.
5. To generate electricity in power plants.

🌡️ Temperature

7 What is temperature?

The temperature of an object is a measurement of the degree of hotness of the object. An object with a high temperature is hot while an object with a low temperature is cold.

Temperature is measured in degree Celsius (°C).

8 What do we use to measure temperature?

Our skin cannot accurately tell the temperature of objects. In order to take exact measurements of the temperature of objects, we use a thermometer.

The following table shows the two types of commonly used thermometers and their features:

Type of Thermometer Uses Features
Clinical Thermometer Used to measure the temperature of the human body. Temperature range: 35°C to 42°C

Has a narrow temperature range as the human body can only stay alive in this narrow temperature range.
Laboratory Thermometer Used to measure temperatures of substances involved in experiments. Temperature range: -10°C to 110°C.

⚖️ Difference between Temperature and Heat

9 What is the difference between temperature and heat?

Temperature is the measurement of the degree of hotness of an object while the amount of heat is the total amount of heat energy contained in an object.

10 What are the factors that affect the amount of heat in an object?

If the type of material is the same, two factors affect the amount of heat it possesses — its temperature and its volume.

Same volume, different temperature:

Beaker A
Volume: 30 ml
Temperature: 25°C
Beaker B
Volume: 30 ml
Temperature: 50°C

The water in beaker B has more heat since the water in both beakers have the same volume (30 ml), but the water in beaker B has higher temperature.

Same temperature, different volume:

Beaker C
Volume: 30 ml
Temperature: 80°C
Beaker D
Volume: 100 ml
Temperature: 80°C

The water in beaker D has more heat since the water in both beakers have the same temperature (80°C), but the water in beaker D has more volume of water.

🔑 Key Takeaway
Among the 4 beakers of water, beaker D has the most heat as it has both the highest temperature (80°C) and the largest volume (100 ml).

🍳 Good and Poor Conductors of Heat

11 What are good conductors and poor conductors of heat?

The heat conductivity of a material measures how easily the material allows heat to flow through it.

🔥 Good Conductor of Heat

Allows heat to flow through it quickly and easily.

Steel Copper Aluminium Iron

❄️ Poor Conductor of Heat

Allows heat to flow through it slowly and not easily.

Plastic Wood Rubber Air

Example: The frying pan

The base of the frying pan must be made of a good conductor of heat such as steel in order to allow heat from the flame to flow to the food faster in order to cook the food faster.

The handle of a frying pan must be made of a poor conductor of heat such as plastic in order to slow down heat flow from the frying pan to the user's hand in order to protect the hand from being burnt by the heat.

⚠️ Common Misconception
Heat conductivity is relative. When two or more materials are given in an experiment, use the words "better" or "poorer" conductors of heat if you are to select them due to their heat conductivity. All metals are good conductors of heat, but some are better conductors than others.

↔️ Expansion and Contraction

12 What happens to a matter when it gains heat or loses heat?

Matter expand when heated and increase in volume. This means that matter occupy more space after gaining heat.

The opposite happens when matter lose heat. When matter lose heat, they contract, occupying less space than before.

The Ball and Ring Experiment:

Initially, the metal ball is able to pass through the ring. After the ball is heated with a flame, it can no longer pass through the iron ring as the iron ball gained heat and expanded, occupying more space than before.

💧 Other Effects of Heat Gain and Heat Loss

Matter can also experience a change in state when they gain heat or lose heat.

Solid
Gain heat → ⇄ ← Lose heat
Liquid
Gain heat → ⇄ ← Lose heat
Gas

When a solid gains heat and the temperature is at the substance's melting point, it will melt, changing from the solid state to liquid state. Ice melts when gaining heat at its melting point of 0°C. Iron melts when gaining heat at its melting point of 1,538°C.

When a liquid gains heat when its temperature is at boiling point, it will boil, changing from the liquid state to the gaseous state. The boiling point of water is 100°C. The boiling point of alcohol is 78.37°C.

When a liquid loses heat when its temperature is at the freezing point, it will freeze, changing from the liquid state to the solid state.

🔑 Key Insight
As the freezing point is also the melting point, what state water ends up in depends on whether it is gaining heat or losing heat. At 0°C, if water is losing heat, it freezes into ice. If ice gains heat at 0°C, it melts.

Even though matter may not be at the boiling or melting/freezing point, it can also undergo state changes.

Liquids such as water evaporate when they gain heat. The liquid slowly changes into a gas at the surface of the liquid.

Gases such as water condense when they lose heat. When these gases come into contact with a sufficiently colder surface, they lose heat and turn into droplets.

🏗️ Effects of Expansion and Contraction in Our Daily Lives

13 What do we do to prevent things from being damaged when they expand or contract after gaining or losing heat?

Humans understand that matter expand after gaining heat and contract after losing heat. Therefore, we build things with the expansion and contraction of materials in mind.

Railway tracks: Gaps are left between each segment of a railway track. This is to prevent them from pushing onto each other and bending each other on a hot day after they have gained heat and expanded.
